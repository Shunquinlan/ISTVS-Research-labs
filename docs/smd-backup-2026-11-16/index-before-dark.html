Soil Machine Dynamics Laboratory · Boone, Iowa

Testing how tires, tracks and tools work the ground

The purpose of the lab is to evaluate machine system performance as equipment interacts with soil, measure soil compaction, and perform wear analysis of ground-engaging tools.

Engineering research for agriculture, construction, mining and extraterrestrial machine applications.

Mission

Mission statement

The mission of the Soil Machine Dynamics Laboratory (SMDL) is to support:

  1. Research programs for off-road equipment design, performance analysis and simulation modeling of mobility systems, including tires, tracks and tractive devices; Ground Engaging Tools (GETs) and GETs wear; and testing services in soil mechanics and tire/track–soil contact area.
  2. Teaching programs for undergraduate and graduate education, with a focus on advanced machinery systems.
  3. Engineering outreach programs on soil–machine systems for tillage, soil compaction and traction.

With these capabilities, the lab supports engineering research for agriculture, construction, mining and extraterrestrial machine applications, and fundamental applied soil dynamics research with various granular materials.

Research

Physical systems modeling and simulation of agricultural and off-road machinery

Methodology & test development

High-fidelity calibration of soil and biomass materials, built on design of experiments, laboratory testing and industrial applications.

Multi-physics modeling

Material-to-machine interactions using FEM, CFD, DEM and multibody dynamics.

Testing of soil–machine systems

Tire and blade interaction, soil stress, soil cutting forces and tire/track forces for model validation.

Data analytics for precision tillage & soil compaction

Measuring and predicting soil compaction and its relationship to yield.
